1On February 28, 1919, a man came to Dr. Schofield’s house. 2A man: Dr. Schofield, I have a special favor to ask you. 3Dr. Schofield: What can I do for you? 4A man: Many people are going to gather tomorrow for the independence of Korea. 5Could you take pictures of the event and share them with the world? 6At 2 p.m. on March 1, Dr. Schofield was hiding in a building and saw a large group of people gathering outside. 7He began to take pictures. 8Dr. Schofield: Unbelievable! 9There are so many people here. 10I hope the world will learn about this important event through my pictures. 11Do you know Seok Hopil? 12His English name was Frank W. Schofield, but he loved his Korean name. 13It sounded similar to his real name, and it also had a good meaning. 14Dr. Schofield was a Canadian doctor, and he first came to Korea in 1916 to teach medicine. 15He wanted to teach in Korean, so he began to learn Korean right away. 16He studied so hard that he was able to teach in Korean after only a few years. 17He believed that every country has the right to be independent, so he helped the Korean independence movement. 18On March 1, 1919, Dr. Schofield heard people shouting for Korean independence in Tapgol Park. 19He took pictures of the event with his camera. 20He then wrote an article about the historic event and sent it to foreign newspapers with his pictures. 21Dr. Schofield’s fight for Korean independence didn’t stop even after the March 1st Movement. 22He continued to write about the terrible situation in Korea for foreign newspapers. 23He was under the watchful eye of the Japanese police, so he had to go back to Canada in 1920. 24In 1958, he returned to Korea at the invitation of the Korean government and never left again. 25For the rest of his life, he worked hard to help Koreans, especially poor students. 26Dr. Schofield died in April 1970, and he was buried in Seoul National Cemetery.
